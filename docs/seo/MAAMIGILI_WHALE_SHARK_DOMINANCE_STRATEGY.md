## 1. Current Maamigili whale-shark authority baseline

**FACT.** Maamigili is essentially invisible in the highest-value search surfaces:
- Google's **Local Pack** (the 3-business map result) for whale-shark/local-island queries surfaced Dhigurah and Fenfushi businesses repeatedly across this research pass — **zero Maamigili businesses appeared in any Local Pack result checked.**
- Of 10 live SERP checks, an **AI Overview appeared on 2** ("South Ari whale shark accommodation," "Dhigurah whale shark snorkeling") — both named Dhigurah/Fenfushi/resort properties; Maamigili appeared only once, as a passing geographic reference ("Villa Park... sits close to Maamigili's famous Whale Shark Point"), never as a place to stay.
- Vilu Residence's own self-authored copy (Facebook bio, homepage) does **not currently contain the phrase "whale shark"** in its core description — a real, verified gap, distinct from the Dhigurah-awareness problem.
- **New, more surprising finding**: at least three *same-island* Maamigili competitors already out-brand Vilu on whale sharks specifically — **Whale Shark Inn** (the term is literally in its business name), **Koimala** (its own site runs a page titled "Maamigili Island – Nature, Adventure & Whale Shark Encounters"), and **Shamar Guest House** (a third-party directory profile titles it "Dive, Whale Sharks & Local Life," and calls Maamigili "the Whale Shark Island" in that same profile).
- The one authority-tier foothold Maamigili already has: **Visit Maldives' own official page names Maamigili** — third in a four-island list ("Dhigurah, Dhidhdhoo, Maamigili, and Dhangethi") — a real, if modest, government-source citation to build on.

**INFERENCE**: the baseline problem is not "Dhigurah vs. Maamigili" alone — it's that Vilu currently under-brands its own strongest asset relative to *its own neighbors*, before even competing with Dhigurah's larger market.

## 2. Authoritative evidence supporting Maamigili

All verified directly against primary sources this pass and last:

- **Harvey-Carroll et al., *Scientific Reports* 2021** (nature.com/articles/s41598-020-79101-8): South Ari Atoll Marine Protected Area (SAMPA) is "one of few locations globally where year-long residency of individuals occurs" — a population almost exclusively immature males, tracked by the Maldives Whale Shark Research Programme.
- **PADI's own official dive-site page for Maamigili Beyru** (padi.com/dive-site/maldives/maamigili-beyru/): names the site directly "Maamigili Beyru (Whale Shark Marine Protected Area)," part of "the South Ari Marine Protected Zone, established to preserve the habitat of whale sharks," with sightings "spotted year-round, especially on sunny days with calm seas."
- **Maldives Ministry of Environment**, SAMPA official designation (protectedareas.environment.gov.mv): confirms the protected-area boundary and habitat-protection basis, already cited on-site.
- **Villa International Airport (VAM)** — Wikipedia, CAPA airport profile: the only airport in South Ari Atoll, confirmed independently across three research passes now.
- **Visit Maldives** (visitmaldives.com): names Maamigili as one of four equivalent whale-shark-region local-island bases.

## 3. Maamigili Beyru findings

**FACT, corrected across two research passes**: Maamigili Beyru is the reef itself — PADI's own naming and description make it a legitimate whale-shark claim, not merely a manta/reef site as an earlier internal draft had it. **Content-gap finding, new this pass**: site-wide, every mention of "Beyru" outside `maamigili-guide.html` itself (in `south-ari-atoll-guide.html`, `whale-shark-snorkeling.html`, `getting-to-maamigili.html`, `holiday-packages.html`) is a single, repeated **manta-ray image alt-text string** — "Manta ray gliding over the reef at Maamigili Beyru" — never once connected to whale sharks in visible body copy anywhere except the one guide page. This is a concrete, low-risk strengthening opportunity: the site's own most-repeated Beyru reference undersells its real, sourced whale-shark identity.

## 4. Strongest safe public claims

- Maamigili Beyru is named and described by PADI itself as a whale-shark site inside the South Ari Marine Protected Area, with year-round sightings.
- South Ari (SAMPA) is one of only a few places globally with documented year-long individual whale-shark residency (precise wording — see §5).
- Maamigili has the only airport in South Ari Atoll.
- Visit Maldives' own page already includes Maamigili among South Ari's whale-shark local-island bases.
- Sightings reported essentially every week of every month (already-approved, cited claim).

## 5. Claims we must avoid

- "Maamigili is the best whale-shark place in the Maldives" — no source supports an exclusive superiority claim.
- "Non-migratory population" / "permanently resident population" — the precise, defensible finding is "year-long residency of individuals" (Harvey-Carroll et al.'s own wording).
- Any claim that Dhigurah is inferior, less authentic, or wrong to choose.
- Sightings "guaranteed," "always," or any percentage figure not stated on a primary source (an "80%" figure surfaced in earlier research from a non-primary source and was correctly excluded from the draft page).
- "Maamigili is an alternative to Dhigurah" — contradicts the standing Maamigili-first positioning rule.

## 6. Current Dhigurah whale-shark search advantage

**FACT, confirmed directly via live SERP this pass**: Dhigurah wins organic results, Local Pack results, and the one clearly-observed AI Overview for a Dhigurah-specific query. Maldives Magazine calls Dhigurah "the textbook whale shark base"; a Facebook forum and a travel blog both independently call it the "best" whale-shark place, by name.

## 7. Why Dhigurah currently has stronger awareness

**INFERENCE, evidence-based**: content volume, not ecological fact. A ~100-guesthouse market on Dhigurah has collectively published far more whale-shark-specific content (dedicated landing pages, dive-site listings, forum recommendations, a YouTube video) than Maamigili's much smaller guesthouse market has. Google's Local Pack and AI Overview mechanisms both reward exactly this kind of content density and local-business-signal volume — they are not evaluating underlying biology.

## 8. How Maamigili can overtake that awareness

**STRATEGIC OPPORTUNITY**, in priority order:
1. **Fix Vilu's own branding first** — the cheapest, fastest win. Whale Shark Inn, Koimala, and Shamar (Maamigili's own neighbors) already out-brand Vilu on this exact topic. Vilu doesn't need to out-publish Dhigurah's 100 guesthouses to close this specific gap — it needs to match what three doors down are already doing.
2. **Close the Local Pack gap** — a Google Business Profile/local-SEO exercise (§18), not a content-writing exercise. This is a different lever from on-page content and appears to be the single most concrete, currently-unaddressed gap found this pass.
3. **Publish the one honest, currently-unserved comparison** — "Dhigurah vs Maamigili" content genuinely does not exist anywhere online today (confirmed this pass). This is a real, low-competition opportunity Vilu can own factually and first, without disparaging Dhigurah.
4. **Strengthen Maamigili Beyru's own whale-shark framing sitewide** (§3) — low-effort, already-sourced, currently understated.
5. **Earn real external citations** (§21) — content alone doesn't move Local Pack or AI-answer weighting as much as independent, cited authority does.
