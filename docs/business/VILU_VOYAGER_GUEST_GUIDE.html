6. Maamigili island information

Maamigili is a real, inhabited local island in South Ari Atoll — home to a working community, not a private resort. It sits inside the South Ari Marine Protected Area (SAMPA), one of a small number of places worldwide where researchers have documented year-long residency of individual whale sharks (Harvey-Carroll et al., Scientific Reports, 2021). Maamigili also has its own domestic airport — the only one in South Ari Atoll — which is why many South Ari-bound travelers, not just those staying in Maamigili, land here first.

Because it's a real village, you'll find local shops, cafés, a mosque, and normal island life alongside the beach and marine side of things. Part of what makes a stay here different from a private-island resort is exactly this — genuine local-island life, not a bubble.

7. Bikini Beach & local dress

Maamigili has a designated Bikini Beach — the one place on the island where resort-style swimwear (bikinis, swim trunks, etc.) is the local norm. Everywhere else on the island — the village, shops, other beach areas — modest dress is expected, in line with local Maldivian custom on inhabited islands. Our team can point you to Bikini Beach on arrival.

8. The alcohol rule

Maldivian local (inhabited) islands, including Maamigili, do not permit alcohol under national law — this applies island-wide, not just at Vilu. Alcohol is only available on resort islands and liveaboard vessels elsewhere in the country. Please don't bring alcohol onto the island.

9. Getting around: transport

Vilu holiday packages include a round-trip speedboat transfer. Please note: domestic flights are never included in a Vilu package — Maamigili's airport is a separate, valuable access point for South Ari, but flight tickets are booked and paid for separately by each guest. If anything about your own transport plan is unclear, ask us before you travel, not after.

Getting around Maamigili itself is easy on foot — it's a small, walkable island. For anything further (day trips, other islands), speak to our team about current options.

13. What to bring

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (protects both you and the marine environment)
  • A rash guard or light long-sleeve top for snorkeling
  • Modest clothing for island areas outside Bikini Beach
  • A light jacket or cover-up for boat transfers (it can be breezy on the water)
  • Any personal medication you need — the nearest well-stocked pharmacy may be off-island

14. Marine safety & wildlife

Please always follow your guide's instructions in the water. Keep a respectful distance from whale sharks, manta rays, and other marine life — no touching, no chasing, no blocking an animal's path. These are wild animals in a protected marine area, and following guide instructions protects both you and them.

Sightings of whale sharks, manta rays, and other marine life are never guaranteed on any specific excursion — this is true everywhere in the Maldives, not just at Vilu.

15. Non-swimmers

Let us know in advance if you or anyone in your group is not a confident swimmer. Life jackets are available for snorkeling excursions, and our team and excursion guides can adjust the experience so everyone can safely take part.

16. Weather

The Maldives has a tropical climate with a wetter monsoon season and a drier season across the year; short showers can happen even in the drier months, and conditions can affect whether a boat excursion goes ahead on a given day. If weather affects a planned excursion, our team will help you reschedule where possible.
