South Ari Atoll Travel Guide

Year-round whale sharks, manta rays, and the local islands that make up one of the Maldives' most distinctive atolls.

South Ari Atoll (locally Alif Dhaalu Atoll) sits in the central Maldives, a ring of reefs and sandbanks built around a string of inhabited local islands and resorts. It's one of 26 atolls that make up the country, and it's best known for one thing above all: whale sharks.

Quick Facts

Location

Central Maldives, about 75km south of Malé.

Atoll Type

Ring atoll — administrative atoll code Alifu Dhaalu.

Best Known For

The only confirmed year-round whale shark aggregation in the world, plus regular manta ray sightings.

Getting There

Speedboat (about 1h45m) or domestic flight (about 20min) from Malé.

Whale sharks, year-round

The South Ari Marine Protected Area (SAMPA), established in 2009, runs along a 14-kilometre reef shelf on the atoll's southern rim, from Maamigili in the west to Dhigurah in the east. Research published by the Maldives Whale Shark Research Programme — which has photo-identified more than 500 individual sharks since 2006 — has documented what's believed to be the only confirmed year-round whale shark aggregation anywhere in the world. Most of the sharks seen here are juvenile males, so sightings aren't tied to a narrow migration window the way they are in many other whale shark destinations.

Manta rays

Manta rays are also a regular sight in South Ari, with sightings possible throughout the year at several cleaning stations around the atoll — some sites are more active earlier in the year, others later, so exact timing is best confirmed locally when you book.

Snorkeling and local island life

Because the whale shark and manta sites here sit close to the surface and close to shore, they're accessible to snorkelers, not just certified divers — one of the things that makes South Ari a practical base for a guesthouse stay rather than only a liveaboard destination. Beyond the marine life, the atoll's inhabited islands each have their own small-town rhythm: fishing, guesthouses, mosques, and tea shops rather than resort infrastructure.

Nearby islands

Dhigurah

"Long Island" in Dhivehi, about 4km long with the atoll's longest beach, plus a sheltered lagoon known for manta ray sightings.

Dhangethi

A small house reef and a compact bikini beach, popular with the diving crowd.

Omadhoo

A quieter fishing island with a clean bikini beach and a reef best visited at high tide.

Mahibadhoo

The atoll's administrative capital, home to the atoll office.

Sample 3-Day Itinerary

Day 1

Arrive, settle in, and get your bearings on the island. Optional sunset cruise in the evening.

Day 2

Whale shark snorkeling in the morning, when the water is calmest, followed by a manta ray cleaning station in the afternoon.

Day 3

A sandbank trip in the morning, time to explore the local island on foot, then departure.

Getting here

Reaching South Ari Atoll means one connection from Velana International Airport in Malé: either a direct speedboat (around 1h45m) or a 20-minute domestic flight into Maamigili.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a diving certification to see the whale sharks or manta rays?
No — every whale shark and manta ray site in South Ari Atoll sits close to the surface, so snorkeling is enough. You don't need to be a certified diver to see either.
What's the best time of year to visit?
Marine life in South Ari is present year-round, so there isn't a single "best" month for wildlife — weather and price are the bigger seasonal factors. See our full Best Time to Visit guide.
Should I stay on a local island or at a resort?
Both are valid choices with real trade-offs in price, privacy, and access to local culture. See our full Guesthouse vs Resort comparison.
How do I get to South Ari Atoll from Malé?
Either a direct speedboat (around 1h45m) or a 20-minute domestic flight into Maamigili's Villa International Airport.
Which island should I base myself on for the best whale shark access?
Maamigili, on the atoll's southern rim, sits closest to the South Ari Marine Protected Area and has its own airport — most whale shark excursions leave directly from there. Read our full Maamigili Guide.
